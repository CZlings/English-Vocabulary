- natural ultradian rhythms
  > An ultradian rhythm is a recurring biological cycle lasting less than 24 hours. These cycles—typically spanning 90 to 120 minutes—regulate vital bodily functions such as hormone release, heart rate, and sleep stages, while also driving the natural ebb and flow of daily focus and energy.Understanding these physiological patterns offers a practical framework for managing cognitive performance and preventing burnout.The 90-Minute Focus CycleThroughout the day, the brain naturally moves through periods of high alertness followed by a dip in energy.The Peak: The first 60 to 90 minutes of the cycle involve an increase in heart rate, hormonal levels, and brain-wave activity, creating a window highly suited for deep, concentrated work.The Trough: As the cycle completes, physical and mental alertness naturally decline, signaling the body's need for a 15 to 20-minute reset. Attempting to power through these natural troughs can lead to fatigue and diminishing returns.Ultradian vs. Circadian RhythmsIn chronobiology, biological cycles are distinguished by their frequency:Ultradian rhythms: Repeat multiple times a day (cycles are shorter than 20 hours, usually ~90 minutes).Circadian rhythms: Complete one cycle roughly every 24 hours, heavily influenced by the sleep-wake cycle and daylight (e.g., the circadian clock).Infradian rhythms: Complete a cycle over a period longer than 24 hours, such as the monthly human menstrual cycle.Sleep CyclesAt night, ultradian rhythms manifest as the progression of sleep stages. A typical adult cycles through non-REM and REM sleep approximately every 90 to 120 minutes. The progression moves from light sleep into deep, slow-wave sleep, and eventually into REM sleep, before repeating the sequence several times throughout the night.Productivity and ManagementTo harness ultradian rhythms for productivity, time management frameworks—like the traditional Pomodoro Technique—are often adapted to align with 90-minute or 60-minute windows. Tracking your personal energy peaks and valleys can help you optimally schedule deep-focus tasks during periods of high cognitive function and schedule restorative breaks during the corresponding lulls
